# Project Osprey / Starbase Louisiana — documented timeline

This index separates **what the 38-page CPRA records production states** from later **official announcements, statutes, permit references, and third-party reporting**. A meeting invitation is not a permit approval; discussion of a city or legislation is not proof that either was ultimately adopted for the project.

## Primary records and provenance

- **CPRA production (38 pages):** [CPRA Records 1 on DocumentCloud](https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/28880366-cpra-records1/?mode=document) — correspondence and attachments involving Louisiana Economic Development (LED), Coastal Protection and Restoration Authority (CPRA), DOTD, USACE and others; March–August 2026. Page references below refer to this PDF's **physical PDF pages**, not separate attachments. This is an **external link**; the file is not mirrored in this repository.
- **Original reporting and document publication:** [Drew Hawkins, Gulf States Newsroom / WWNO, November 11, 2026](https://www.wwno.org/economy/2026-11-11/a-new-city-for-spacex-in-louisiana-records-reveal-the-scale-of-officials-plans). Credit belongs to the reporter who obtained/published the source materials.
- **Limitations:** Substantial redactions, including obscured names, technical scopes and near-blank pages. Dates of communications do not establish dates of approvals, executed agreements or expenditure.

## Chronology: what the released CPRA records establish

| Date | Documentary event | Source pages | What it establishes / does not |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **March 12, 2026** | LED's Paige Carter requests an urgent, confidential Project Osprey briefing with CPRA, DOTD and counsel; circulates a sensitive briefing marked attorney-client privilege. | **4–6** | Senior-agency project coordination was occurring. A privilege label does not independently establish the legal basis for redactions. |
| **March 12** | Project Osprey briefing identifies (1) a large launch site and (2) **manufacturing of launch vehicles**; describes ExxonMobil land subject to a reported long-term hunting lease and contemplated negotiations. | **6** | Manufacturing was contemplated before public announcement; the document does not prove that manufacturing construction was approved. |
| **March 12** | Under “anticipated needs to win,” the briefing lists possible **noise/nuisance legislation** and **construction of a new city in Vermilion Parish**; details about the proposed city are redacted. | **6** | The state considered these possibilities; no incorporation application or adopted municipal plan is established. |
| **March 12** | Briefing also mentions **orbital data centers**, NASA Moon commitments, and other proposed project drivers. | **6** | The phrase concerns stated project rationale, not proof of a terrestrial Louisiana data center. |
| **March 13** | Carter circulates LED research on Starbase, Texas. The slides describe employee housing, incorporation and Texas highway/beach closure powers. | **8, 11–21**, particularly **12, 15** | LED examined the Texas precedent. Texas law does **not** confer equivalent powers in Louisiana. |
| **April 6** | Carter sends DOTD and CPRA project material for evaluation of access, shoreline and other site-related matters, with changed anticipated property boundaries. | **22–32** | Early infrastructure evaluation; the included technical materials are heavily redacted or not legible. |
| **April 22** | Carter circulates an updated Project Osprey briefing, instructing recipients not to use the company name. The distribution includes agency officials, utilities, One Acadiana and Royal Engineering. | **33–34** | Demonstrates organized, confidential coordination and Royal's involvement by this date; does not establish procurement terms. |
| **April 22** | Revised briefing describes **phase one** launch-site minimum capital expenditure of **$20 billion** and **3,000 jobs**, with later phases dependent on phase-one investment; lists **multi-bill legislation** and extensive DOTD, USACE and power requirements. | **34** | These were planning figures and needs, not proof of final contractual investment or enacted bills. |
| **July 8–9** | LED circulates a USACE meeting agenda and repeats that the **company requested** the Project Osprey alias. A multi-agency meeting is scheduled for July 9. | **35–38** | Establishes meeting planning and participants, not necessarily every attendee's actual presence. |
| **July 9** | Meeting agenda proposes coordination of permits, Section 408 authorizations and consideration of a **Section 214 agreement** to facilitate federal review. | **37–38** | Section 214 was a discussion objective; this record does **not** establish an agreement was signed or any federal standard waived. |
| **July 27–31 / Aug. 4** | Follow-up emails circulate presentations/materials and schedule an in-person company/USACE/state-agency meeting for **August 4**. | **1–3** | Continued coordination. The production alone does not establish the August 4 meeting's outcome. |
